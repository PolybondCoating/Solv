# Powder defect claim verification: craters/fish eyes, orange peel, sags, adhesion, corrosion, oven-temperature fix

Scope note: about 17 tool calls used. Fetched pages are quoted from the fetch tool's extracts, which are partly paraphrased summaries of the pages, not raw text. Items marked "(summary)" below are paraphrases from the fetch tool and not verbatim. Page numbers are not available for web pages; the publisher and section are given instead. The Qualicoat figures come from a copy of the spec on a council planning server (camdocs.camden.gov.uk), not from qualicoat.net, so re-check them against the current official PDF. I did not reach TIGER Drylac, Jotun, Interpon (AkzoNobel), PPG, Sherwin-Williams powder, ISO or ASTM documents themselves. Nothing I found says anything specific to Indian conditions.

## 1. Craters / fish eyes

### Takeaway
Contaminant causes (oil, silicone, dirty compressed air, poor pretreatment) are CONFIRMED by Axalta, PCI and Powder Coating magazine. The report's numbers (ISO 8573-1 oil Class 1 / water Class 4, dyne 42-44 mN/m, filter change every 3-6 months) have NO primary source and stay UNSOURCED. The only air numbers I found are Nordson's, and they are not ISO class numbers. "Fish eyes are NOT caused by thick film": no source lists film thickness as a cause, so this is neither confirmed nor contradicted. I rate it UNSOURCED, leaning supported.

### Cited Findings
- Oil/silicone/compressed-air cause: CONFIRMED. Axalta Powder Coatings (US) FAQ "Problems on the coated object", Craters. Causes include "Objects not sufficiently degreased, pre-treated", "Influences of products containing silicones", oil contamination in compressed air and oven condensation (summary). Remedies: better degreasing/pretreatment, avoid silicones, use oil separators. https://www.axalta.com/powder_us/en_US/technical/frequently-asked-questions/problems-on-the-coated-object.html
- PCI (Powder Coating Institute, via Powder Coating magazine) "PC Summitry: Achieving Powder Cure and Avoiding Craters": craters are "a circular interruption in a coating surface that is a result of a contaminant that has a significantly different surface tension." The most prominent cause is "unclean compressed air in your spray equipment". Other sources: compressor lubricant, WD-40, greases, rust preventatives, "forming oils, drawing compounds and press lubricants", and silicones or acrylics mixed into standard powders. The extract did not mention film thickness or outgassing. https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/349/PC-Summitry-Achieving-Powder-Cure-and-Avoiding-Craters
- Chemquest 2025 webinar (powder-industry training deck, secondary): "Craters are always caused by contaminants with lower surface tension than the powder coating". Fisheyes have "a visible particle at the base of the crater". Lists cross-contamination, liquid-coating residue, processing oils and personal-care silicone. https://chemquest.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Understanding-and-Preventing-Powder-Coating-Defects-2025-webinar.pdf
- Powder Coating magazine "Ask Joe": silicone paint has about 15-20 dynes/cm against powder at 40-50 dynes/cm. As little as 0.001% silicone can cause widespread cratering. Silicone mist spreads through compressed-air systems, booth exhaust and oven air handling. https://pcimag.com/articles/94568-ask-joe-powder-3
- Air quality, the only hard numbers found: Nordson Econo-Coat Series II manual (part 106712C02, quoted by paintbooth.com): pressure "4.1-6.89 bar (60-100 psi)", "pressure dew point of -6.7 to 3.4 C (20 to 38 F), or lower", and oil vapor or aerosol "no more than 0.1 ppm" (filtration 99% efficient above 0.3 micron). This is a manufacturer figure for one gun series and is not expressed as ISO 8573-1 classes. The paintbooth.com article names ISO 8573-1:2010 only as a framework and gives no class numbers for powder. https://www.paintbooth.com/compressed-air-powder-coating-gun-hopper/
- The Nordson dew point of about -7 to 3 C is looser than an ISO 8573-1 water Class 4. Class 4 is +3 C, from my memory of the standard and not fetched. That is consistent with "Class 4", but I have no powder-specific source stating it.

### Inferences
- Nordson's 0.1 ppm oil limit would be tighter than ISO 8573-1 oil Class 2 (0.1 mg/m3 is my recollection, unverified). I cannot confirm "Class 1" from any powder source. Treat it as unsourced and advise applicators to follow the gun maker's manual.
- Nothing I found explains why dyne readings of 42-44 would be the pass level, and dyne pens are not commonly cited for metal pretreatment. Treat the claim as UNSOURCED.

### Gaps
- No source found for the ISO 8573-1 oil Class 1 / water Class 4 claim for powder application.
- No source found for the dyne pen 42-44 mN/m claim. McMaster and kolorguide results were product or print-industry pages, not coating-industry guidance.
- No source found for "change filters every 3-6 months". Nordson only says "check the dryer and filters".
- Film thickness and craters: this is not covered by the sources fetched. Many sources link thick films to gassing/pinholes, but I did not confirm that for this report.

## 2. Orange peel

### Takeaway
Thick film, a slow heat-up and old or pre-reacted powder are CONFIRMED as causes. The "parts about 40-50 C entering spray" claim is DISPUTED: the only sourced figure is "less than 40 C", and it is an upper limit for parts leaving the dry-off oven. The "target plus no more than 20%" fix and "sanding fixes appearance" have no source and are UNSOURCED.

### Cited Findings
- Products Finishing (Sames article, "How to avoid orange peel in powder coating"): "Your film thickness is too high or too low." "The heat up cycle of your parts is too slow." "The powder has been store too long or it has pre-reacted. ... If powder is too old, exchange with fresher powder." Substrate should not exceed 40 C (104 F), and "Parts are too hot when coming from the dry-off oven. Try allowing a longer cooler time (less than 40ºC/104ºF for parts." https://www.pfonline.com/articles/how-to-avoid-orange-peel-in-powder-coating
- Axalta FAQ, Orange Peel (summary): film thickness too high or low, non-optimal temperature profile, inappropriate powder (too reactive, too high-viscosity), particle size distribution, back ionisation from excessive voltage, poor storage. https://www.axalta.com/powder_us/en_US/technical/frequently-asked-questions/problems-on-the-coated-object.html
- PCI "Ask the Expert" May/June 2026 (Powder Coating magazine): "Orange peel is rarely caused by powder chemistry alone. In most cases, it results from an imbalance between film build, electrostatic application and cure dynamics." Fixes: reduce film build, lower gun voltage, consistent overlap, and profile part temperature. "Air temperature alone is not a reliable indicator; profiling actual part metal temperature is essential." https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/5487/Ask-the-Expert-MayJune-2026
- Chemquest webinar: low film build often goes with MORE orange peel. This conflicts with the simple "thick film causes it" story. https://chemquest.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Understanding-and-Preventing-Powder-Coating-Defects-2025-webinar.pdf

### Inferences
- Where sources disagree: the report's "40-50 C entering spray" against the "40 C" figure. The Sames source says "less than 40 C" and gives it as a limit, not a range. A 40-50 C range exceeds that limit. The sources are also not describing the same thing: the Sames point concerns parts leaving a dry-off oven, which is not necessarily the temperature at the spray booth entrance.
- Sources disagree on film thickness: Sames and Axalta say "too high or low", the report says only too thick. Chemquest says low build shows more orange peel.
- The Sames text is itself loosely written (the 40 C line is a partial sentence), so it is a weak primary source. No manufacturer data sheet was found for a part-temperature limit.

### Gaps
- No source for "target + no more than 20%".
- No source for "sanding fixes appearance". Orange peel is cured-in texture, so sanding and recoating is a repair step, not a process fix. I found no source either way.

## 3. Sags / drips

### Takeaway
Too-thick or uneven film is CONFIRMED. "Parts too hot (below 40 C)" is DISPUTED and apparently garbled: sources tie sags to cure-profile conditions, not to a 40 C part temperature. "Raise voltage and ground better" is UNSOURCED for sags, and a source on orange peel points the other way.

### Cited Findings
- Axalta FAQ, Sagging/Drop Formation (summary): "Film thickness too high or variable" and "Curing conditions not optimal (heating rate, object and oven temperature)". Also object geometry and powder specification. Remedies: standardise film thickness, optimise heating rates and temperatures. https://www.axalta.com/powder_us/en_US/technical/frequently-asked-questions/problems-on-the-coated-object.html
- Axalta lists back ionisation from excessive voltage under orange peel, and PCI says lower gun voltage for orange peel. Raising voltage is therefore not a sourced fix, and it can add film build and back ionisation. https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/5487/Ask-the-Expert-MayJune-2026
- PCI article says poor grounding is a cause of orange peel (Chemquest deck: "poor grounding"). That is not a sag source. https://chemquest.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Understanding-and-Preventing-Powder-Coating-Defects-2025-webinar.pdf

### Inferences
- "Parts too hot (below 40 C)" is internally contradictory as written. In the Sames source the 40 C figure is a maximum for parts leaving dry-off, given for orange peel, not sags. The report likely mixed up its sources.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer source found stating a sag-specific part temperature. Sherwin-Williams results were liquid-paint pages and are not relevant to powder.

## 4. Poor adhesion

### Takeaway
Pretreatment failure and under-cure as causes are CONFIRMED. "Over-cure" is CONFIRMED only in a narrow sense (over-cure of a FIRST coat in a recoat system). Cross-cut ISO 2409 is CONFIRMED as the Qualicoat method with a result of 0. ASTM D3359 ratings and "iron phosphate for steel, zinc phosphate for galvanized" are UNSOURCED here. Flash rust was not found as a cause.

### Cited Findings
- Axalta FAQ, Adhesion Issues (summary): "Film thickness beyond nominal values", "Insufficiently cross-linked powder", "Insufficient pre-treatment of objects (phosphating, chromating)", and "Overcuring of first coatings". https://www.axalta.com/powder_us/en_US/technical/frequently-asked-questions/problems-on-the-coated-object.html
- PCI "Troubleshooting Field Defects": "A high-quality powder coating properly applied and cured over a clean, pretreated substrate should not suffer adhesion failures." Pretreatment must be in spec for temperature, concentration, pH, TDS, exposure time and impingement. "Follow the recommended bake conditions (time and temperature) for each specific powder. This can be found on the TDS". https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/5256/Troubleshooting-Field-Defects
- Qualicoat Specifications 2023 (copy at camdocs.camden.gov.uk, a secondary host): cross-cut adhesion, ISO 2409 tape test per section 2.4.1, "The result shall be 0". https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/10271052/file/document
- PCI Architectural Spec Comparison (PCI): Qualicoat film adhesion "Min Gt-0 ISO 2409 6 parallel of 1 mm/min Gt-0 Scotch 610". https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.powdercoating.org/resource/resmgr/docs/PCI_Architectural_Spec_Compa.pdf
- PCI cure-check method: acetone double rubs, "Polyesters: withstand 25 acetone double rubs with minimal color transfer". https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/349/PC-Summitry-Achieving-Powder-Cure-and-Avoiding-Craters
- Undercure can hide: "the coating can 'look' cured but, in fact, be undercured", with brittleness and compromised adhesion. https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/4052/categoryId/4/Technology-InterchangeHow-Oven-Conditions-Affect-Powder-Coating-Performance

### Inferences
- Qualicoat is an aluminium architectural spec and its Gt 0 requirement should not be quoted as the general acceptance level for steel work in India. Data sheet and customer spec rule.
- The Axalta cause "film thickness beyond nominal values" gives a thick-film route to adhesion loss. Chemquest and PCI also say avoid excessively thick coatings.

### Gaps
- No source fetched for ASTM D3359 rating scale (5B-0B or 5A-0A) and no source for iron phosphate vs zinc phosphate by substrate. Axalta only says "phosphating, chromating".
- No source for flash rust as an adhesion cause.

## 5. Corrosion under film

### Takeaway
Pretreatment failure, thin film, edges and under-cure are CONFIRMED as causes. The "at least 60 um" thin-film figure matches Qualicoat but only for architectural aluminium, and it should not be treated as a general minimum (data sheet wins). "Under 3 mm creep after 500 h" has NO source and is DISPUTED by what Qualicoat actually states. "Pinholes" and "primer" are UNSOURCED here.

### Cited Findings
- PCI "Troubleshooting Field Defects": "Incomplete coverage due to thin films and/or poor coverage of edges can lead to early corrosion failures." "Marginal or poor adhesion will lead to scissions or breaks in the coating, which will invite moisture and salt to accelerate corrosion." "Ensure that the film thickness on all surfaces is at, or above, the minimum thickness recommended on the product's TDS." https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/5256/Troubleshooting-Field-Defects
- Axalta FAQ, Corrosion Resistance: primary cause "Insufficiently cross-linked powder"; solution "Check crosslinking/curing, comply with curing conditions". https://www.axalta.com/powder_us/en_US/technical/frequently-asked-questions/problems-on-the-coated-object.html
- Qualicoat Specifications 2023 (copy): minimum film thickness "60 µm" for powder Classes 1, 1.5 and 2, and "50 µm" for Class 3 powder (section 2.3). "None of the measured values shall be less than 80% of the specified minimum value." Acetic-acid salt spray (section 2.10): "1000 hours" for standard coatings, "2000 hours" for Class 3. Filiform corrosion (section 2.19): "L (longest filament) ≤ 4 mm" and "M (average length of filaments) ≤ 2 mm" within 10 cm on each side of the scratch. Final rinse conductivity max 30 µS/cm. https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/10271052/file/document
- PCI Architectural Spec Comparison: Qualicoat filiform "Within 10 cm each side of scratch: L ≤ 4 mm M ≤ 2 mm" (Classes 2 and 3). AAMA 2604-21 "ASTM G85 1500 h Few No. 8 blisters No removal of film"; AAMA 2605-20 "ASTM G85 2000 h Few No. 8 blisters No removal of film". https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.powdercoating.org/resource/resmgr/docs/PCI_Architectural_Spec_Compa.pdf

### Inferences
- The report's "ISO 9227 / ASTM B117 hours" needs care. Qualicoat's salt-spray test is acetic-acid salt spray at 1000 h (2000 h for Class 3). That is a different test from neutral salt spray ASTM B117, and AAMA uses ASTM G85. Do not mix them.
- The "under 3 mm after 500 h" creep figure does not match any value I found. Qualicoat's numbers are 4 mm (L) and 2 mm (M) for filiform at its own test duration, and the salt-spray creep limit was not captured. Treat it as unverified, and do not quote it.

### Gaps
- Qualicoat's salt-spray creep limit (mm at scribe) was not captured in my extract. Check section 2.10 of the official spec.
- No source on pinholes and corrosion, or on primer use (zinc-rich or epoxy primers).
- No AkzoNobel/Interpon, PPG, TIGER or Jotun creep limits found.

## 6. Raising oven temperature as a fix

### Takeaway
Sources say the opposite of a blanket "raise oven temperature": verify metal temperature against the data sheet first. The claim as written is DISPUTED.

### Cited Findings
- PCI: "the time and temperature stated refers to the part temperature and not the oven air temperature". Verify with data loggers (DatPaq, Curvex-2) or IR pyrometers. https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/349/PC-Summitry-Achieving-Powder-Cure-and-Avoiding-Craters
- PCI Technology Interchange: "When a TDS recommends 15 minutes at 375°F, you must ensure that all your coated parts reach at least 375°F and stay at or above that temperature for 15 minutes." Oven recovery after loading "does not accurately indicate the part temperature." https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/4052/categoryId/4/Technology-InterchangeHow-Oven-Conditions-Affect-Powder-Coating-Performance
- PCI Ask the Expert 2026: "Air temperature alone is not a reliable indicator; profiling actual part metal temperature is essential." https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/5487/Ask-the-Expert-MayJune-2026
- Axalta: for sags and orange peel the remedy is to optimise heating rates and profile. https://www.axalta.com/powder_us/en_US/technical/frequently-asked-questions/problems-on-the-coated-object.html

### Inferences
- Raising oven setpoint can fix under-cure caused by slow heat-up (heavy sections), but it can also speed the gel, reduce flow and worsen orange peel (Axalta lists "too reactive" and a poor temperature profile as orange-peel causes). It is a fix only after a profile shows the parts missed the data-sheet metal temperature and time.

### Gaps
- No source quantifying how much oven temperature may be raised.

## Where sources disagree / report numbers with no source

Disagreements:
1. Part temperature at spray entry: report 40-50 C vs source "less than 40 C" (Sames/Products Finishing, a dry-off exit limit).
2. Film thickness and orange peel: "too thick" (report, PCI 2026) vs "too high or too low" (Axalta, Sames) vs low build gives more orange peel (Chemquest).
3. Sags: "parts too hot" (report) vs Axalta "film thickness too high or variable" and "heating rate".
4. Raise voltage (report, for sags) vs lower voltage for orange peel (Axalta, PCI).
5. Raise oven temperature (report) vs verify metal temperature first (PCI).
6. Corrosion creep "under 3 mm after 500 h" vs Qualicoat's actual limits of 4 mm / 2 mm and 1000 h acetic-acid salt spray.

Unsourced report numbers: ISO 8573-1 oil Class 1 / water Class 4; dyne 42-44 mN/m; filter change 3-6 months; "40-50 C" entering spray; "target + 20%"; "sanding fixes appearance"; "under 3 mm after 500 h"; "iron phosphate for steel / zinc phosphate for galvanized"; ASTM D3359 ratings; ISO 9227 / ASTM B117 hours.

Confirmed with sources: contaminant causes of craters; thick or uneven film for sags; thin film and edges for corrosion; under-cure for adhesion and corrosion; Qualicoat 60 um, 1000 h AASS, filiform 4/2 mm and ISO 2409 result 0 (via secondary copy).
