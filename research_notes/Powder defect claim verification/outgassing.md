# Outgassing defects in powder coating: verification of six claims (India, thermoset powder, metal-temperature-first, data sheet wins)

Method note: pages were read via a fetch tool that returns summarised text, so quoted wording is as returned and should be spot-checked against the live page before final publication. No manufacturer data sheet or standard states the "260 C / 5-10 min / max 290 C" numbers. Unit conversions: 500 F = 260 C; 535 F = 279 C; 450 F = 232 C; 65 F difference = 36 C; 30 F = 17 C.

## Claim 1: Pre-bake (degas) castings and galvanized parts at ~260 C (500 F) for 5-10 min; do not exceed 290 C

### Takeaway
UNSOURCED as stated. No source found gives 260 C / 5-10 min / 290 C max. Sources agree the pre-bake should be at or above the cure temperature, but the offsets (+15 to +36 C), times (15-30 min up to ~1 hour, held until the PART reaches temperature) and ceilings (AGA 535 F = 279 C with phosphate; one casting source 450 F = 232 C) differ. The 5-10 min figure conflicts with every source that gives a time.

### Cited Findings
- AGA (American Galvanizers Association), galvanized: pre-bake oven "approximately 65°F higher than the curing oven, typically not exceeding 535°F if phosphate treatment was applied"; "The part should be pre-baked until the galvanized surface reaches the oven temperature. Typically, this is one hour to assure all moisture and entrapped gasses are expelled." — [AGA Knowledgebase](https://galvanizeit.org/knowledgebase/article/powder-coating-adhesion-and-outgassing); same wording on the AGA-linked [hotdipgalvanizing.com](https://www.hotdipgalvanizing.com/technical-resources/powder-coating-adhesion-and-outgassing-galvanized-steel) (there: "must not exceed 535 degrees Fahrenheit", because higher temperature degrades the phosphate layer). Status: partly DISPUTES claim 1 (the ceiling is ~279 C, not 290 C, and only when phosphated; dwell is until part temperature is reached, ~1 h, not 5-10 min).
- AGA also says to cool to a temperature allowing application "in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions" and coat "as soon as possible so there is little or no time for zinc oxidation to begin"; rushing cooling "can cause... poor powder flow and uneven film build" (hotdipgalvanizing.com page above).
- ASTM D7803 (standard practice for preparing HDG for powder coating) requires a thermal pretreatment; its significance text: "If these volatile materials are not removed through an outgassing process prior to the baking of the powder, then pinholing or blistering can occur." Store page shows no temperature figures (paywalled) — [ASTM D7803-25](https://store.astm.org/d7803-25.html). AGA says only that "D7803 gives guidance for oven temperatures for preheating" — [AGA D6386 vs D7803](https://galvanizeit.org/knowledgebase/article/difference-between-astm-d6386-and-astm-d7803). Exact D7803 numbers: not retrieved.
- Powder Coating Institute (PCI) magazine, Powder Coated Tough, "Dealing with Outgassing": heat parts "20 to 30 degrees Fahrenheit higher than the recommended bake temperature" (about +11 to +17 C), confirm the PART (not oven air) reaches it. — [PCI](https://powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/5239/Dealing-with-OutgassingRoot-Causes-and-Mitigation-Techniques)
- TIGER Drylac (manufacturer): "Pre-heat substrates at least 20°C above the Part Metal Temperature specified in technical data sheets". — [TIGER blog](https://www.tiger-coatings.com/about/tiger-blog/outgassing-in-powder-coating). A TIGER-quoted article (Products Finishing, Denerville) says 15 C (30 F) above PMT — [PF](https://pfonline.com/articles/outgassing-and-powder-coating-defects). Internal TIGER inconsistency: +20 C vs +15 C.
- TIGER Drylac, iron castings: oven "slightly higher than normal curing parameters, but no greater than 450°F"; "400-420°F with a dwell period of 45-50 minutes", adjusted for thickness. — [TIGER blog](https://www.tiger-coatings.com/ca-en/tiger-group/tiger-blog/how-to-prevent-outgassing-of-iron-castings). Closest manufacturer figure to 260 C is 204-216 C, with a 232 C cap; it conflicts with 260 C / 290 C.
- Columbia Coatings (powder distributor/applicator, not a powder maker): pre-bake "at least the cure temperature or 15-20 degrees above the cure temperature" for "15 to 30 minutes"; sequence Clean, Strip/Blast, Outgas, Conversion coat. — [Columbia Coatings](https://www.columbiacoatings.com/?p=134074). Note: the units are not stated in the fetched text (probably F). Secondary source.
- Products Finishing Q&A (Nick Liberto): pre-bake "at a temperature higher than your cure temperature for the same or longer time frame of your cure cycle." — [PF Outgassing Prevention](https://pfonline.com/articles/outgassing-prevention). Die-cast Q&A: some shops preheat "approximately 50°F above ultimate cure temperature, then cool before coating." — [PF die-cast](https://www.pfonline.com/articles/how-to-stop-outgassing-with-die-cast-parts) (about +28 C).
- Products Finishing (Talbert): preheating above cure temperature gives results "ranging from very good to not effective at all"; lower cure temperature helps. — [PF castings](https://pfonline.com/articles/how-to-limit-outgassing-on-castings)
- Freilacke (coatings maker, German): "Pre-heating... prevents gas escape"; mentions low-temperature powders as a risk reducer, no figures. — [Freilacke](https://www.freilacke.com/coating-knowledge/outgassing-during-powder-coating/)

### Inferences
- The only common rule is "part metal temperature at least cure temperature, plus roughly 15-36 C, held until the part (not the oven air) is at temperature". For a Indian shop, 260 C is only sensible if the powder's cure window and the pretreatment tolerate it; with phosphate, AGA caps at 279 C, so the claimed 290 C ceiling is not supported.
- A 5-10 min dwell is likely too short for thick castings and hot-dip galvanized sections; all sourced times are 15 min or more, with AGA ~1 h for galvanized.
- 260 C may have been derived by converting a 500 F figure from an unsourced or forum origin; no primary source for it was found.

### Gaps
- Exact D7803 oven temperatures/time (paywalled).
- No Interpon, PPG, Axalta, Jotun or Sherwin-Williams document with a numeric pre-bake found; no Nordson pre-bake figure.
- No source for "do not exceed 290 C".

## Claim 2: Sweep blast to SSPC-SP16, then zinc-compatible phosphate/pretreatment, then pre-bake

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED in substance with sequence caveats. Sweep blasting per SSPC-SP 16 and zinc-suitable phosphate are both recommended, and the pre-bake follows surface preparation. Sources differ on whether the pre-bake comes before or after blasting/conversion coating (see disagreements).

### Cited Findings
- AGA: SSPC-SP 16 "Brush-Off Blast Cleaning" removes metal oxides and opens "surface cavities that have trapped air"; "A phosphate wash treatment can be employed" to remove oxidation; then pre-bake. — [AGA](https://galvanizeit.org/knowledgebase/article/powder-coating-adhesion-and-outgassing)
- SP 16 surface: free of "visible oil, grease dirt, dust, metal oxides (corrosion products)", "minimum profile of 0.75 mils" (as given on AGA page). — [AGA Brush-Off Blast](https://galvanizeit.org/knowledgebase/article/brush-off-blast-cleaning). Standard: [AMPP/SSPC SP 16 store](https://store.ampp.org/sspc-sp-16-2010-brush-off-blast-cleaning-of-coated-and-uncoated-galvanized-steel-stainless-steels-and-non-ferrous-metals)
- AGA/hotdipgalvanizing.com: SP 16 is "not aggressive abrasive blasting", removes oxides "without cutting through the zinc layer"; phosphate must be "specifically formulated for zinc surfaces" because "standard iron phosphate systems developed for bare steel may react aggressively with zinc". — [hotdipgalvanizing.com](https://www.hotdipgalvanizing.com/technical-resources/powder-coating-adhesion-and-outgassing-galvanized-steel)
- ASTM D7803: the thermal pretreatment is performed "after surface preparation". — [AGA duplex prep](https://galvanizeit.org/knowledgebase/article/surface-preparation-for-duplex-systems)
- AkzoNobel Interpon APA151 anti-gassing primer data sheet: for galvanized steel, "Sweeping with a maximum zinc layer thickness reduction of 5 to 10 µm"; cast steel: grit blast Sa 2.5 plus degrease and phosphate. — [Interpon APA151 TDS](https://powdercoatings.brand.akzonobel.com/m/58001c8a40a13adf/original/APA151-Primer-FL151F.pdf). Note: Interpon says "sweeping", not SSPC-SP16 by name.
- Galvanizers Association of Australia: zinc phosphate has been "preferred pre-treatment" over HDG, "1-2 g/m²". — [GAA Powder coating over galvanised](https://www.steel.org.au/getattachment/44133ac4-bf76-4a27-9c29-120e519d3436/Powder-coating-over-galvanised-)

### Inferences
- SSPC-SP16 is a US-origin standard; an Indian shop can specify it, or use "sweep blast" with the maker's limit (Interpon: remove at most 5-10 µm zinc). ISO 8501/Sa grades do not apply to zinc.

### Gaps
- No PPG/Axalta/Jotun sweep-blast wording retrieved.
- No source found requiring "pre-bake after phosphate" at a specific interval beyond D7803/AGA ordering.

## Claim 3: Reduce film thickness; outgassing-grade (degassing/OGF) powders or primers

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED. Thin films, OGF/degassing powders and anti-gassing primers are all recommended by manufacturers. Specific mil limits come only from a PCI article; maker TDS give a primer range of 60-80 µm.

### Cited Findings
- PCI: keep film "under 5.0 mils and preferably less than 4.0 mils" (about 127 µm / 100 µm) for polyester/urethane powders; "OGF" powders contain waxy additives that let gas escape. — [PCI](https://powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/5239/Dealing-with-OutgassingRoot-Causes-and-Mitigation-Techniques)
- TIGER Drylac lists excessive film thickness as a cause and recommends OGF powders; also OGF primer, OGF top-coats and dry-blend OGF additive (Series 91 OGF Additive TDS; PDF redirected to a PIM download and was not read). — [TIGER blog](https://www.tiger-coatings.com/about/tiger-blog/outgassing-in-powder-coating); [TIGER iron castings](https://www.tiger-coatings.com/ca-en/tiger-group/tiger-blog/how-to-prevent-outgassing-of-iron-castings)
- Interpon APA151: "Anti-gassing primer especially designed for direct application on substrates that are susceptible to out-gassing" (HDG, zamak, cast steel/aluminium); "Recommended film thickness 60-80 µm"; cure 160 C/10-60 min, 180 C/7-40 min, 200 C/5-30 min. — [APA151 TDS](https://powdercoatings.brand.akzonobel.com/m/58001c8a40a13adf/original/APA151-Primer-FL151F.pdf)
- GAA: degassing grades of polyester powder contain additives that "delay the onset of fusion of the powder, which gives more time for entrained gas to escape from the molten polymer film." — [GAA](https://www.steel.org.au/getattachment/44133ac4-bf76-4a27-9c29-120e519d3436/Powder-coating-over-galvanised-)
- PF (Talbert): primer plus topcoat reduces outgassing impact but "adds a lot of cost". — [PF](https://pfonline.com/articles/how-to-limit-outgassing-on-castings). PCI "Outgassing in Powder Coatings": apply two thinner coats instead of one heavy; IR or IR/UV cure; vents/chills. — [PCI](https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/395/Outgassing-in-Powder-Coatings)
- Lower cure temperature / longer time helps gas escape: AGA ("as low as possible"), PF die-cast (with AAMA 2605 limits), PF Talbert.

### Inferences
- OGF/degassing powders are a complement to, not a substitute for, pre-bake on severe castings.

### Gaps
- No quantified thickness limits from a powder maker's general guide; no India-specific OGF product list verified.

## Claim 4: Powder moisture causes scattered pinholes; RH limits for powder storage and booth

### Takeaway
PARTLY CONFIRMED. Sherwin-Williams lists pinholes among moisture defects and gives 40-60 % RH, 50-80 F storage; Teknos gives 40-60 % RH and max 25 C. A booth RH figure of 40-60 % comes from PCI/PF. Whether moisture causes pinholes "scattered" is general; moisture-origin pinholes are not clearly distinguished from substrate outgassing in the sources.

### Cited Findings
- Sherwin-Williams: "Ideal storage conditions range from 50° F to 80° F and 40% to 60% relative humidity"; moisture causes poor adhesion/clumping and "pinholes, cratering, and fisheyes"; use air dryers/filters. No booth RH or dew point given. — [Sherwin-Williams](https://industrial.sherwin-williams.com/na/us/en/general-industrial/resources/moisture-powder.html)
- Teknos (INFRALIT) storage: "max 25 °C"; "recommended (air) humidity during storage and handling of powder coatings is 40-60%"; let cold boxes reach ambient before opening. — [Teknos PDF](https://www.teknos.com/globalassets/teknos.com/industrial-coatings/downloads/brochures/powder-coatings-brochures/infralit_powder_coatings_storage_and_handling_-instructions.pdf)
- PF: "The ideal range of humidity is between 40 and 60 percent" for application; above 60 % powder agglomerates and fluidizes poorly; below 40 % charging problems. — [PF humidity](https://pfonline.com/articles/humidity-levels-for-powder-application)
- Nordson troubleshooting guide: moisture in compressed air causes poor fluidization; "Check room ambient air humidity"; no numeric RH or dew point. — [Nordson PWL3292](https://emanuals.nordson.com/finishing/files/Powder/PWL3292.pdf)
- Freilacke: residual moisture in substrate or powder vaporises and causes bubbles/craters. — [Freilacke](https://www.freilacke.com/coating-knowledge/outgassing-during-powder-coating/)

### Inferences
- India monsoon: 40-60 % RH is hard to hold; dehumidified powder store and booth air are justified by these figures. Dew-point limit for compressed air not found.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer figure for compressed-air dew point; no Interpon/PPG/Axalta storage sheet read (storage figures are from Sherwin-Williams and Teknos).

## Claim 5: Thick sections of castings and welds trap gas; vent holes

### Takeaway
PARTLY CONFIRMED. Castings (porosity) and galvanized work are widely named as gas sources; vents are named only for foundry molds/casting design and "vents/chills". Welds are named by one distributor only; sealed hollow sections/weld crevices for galvanizing are not covered by the powder sources read.

### Cited Findings
- Castings trap gas from pouring/cooling: [PF](https://pfonline.com/articles/outgassing-and-powder-coating-defects), [PCI](https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/395/Outgassing-in-Powder-Coatings), [PF Liberto](https://pfonline.com/articles/outgassing-prevention) ("inherent to all castings"; work with foundry on "molds, patterns, and venting systems").
- PCI: "add vents/chills to problem areas" as a substrate change. — [PCI](https://www.powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/395/Outgassing-in-Powder-Coatings)
- Columbia Coatings: welded assemblies listed among susceptible parts. — [Columbia](https://www.columbiacoatings.com/?p=134074)
- Impregnation/resin sealing of porosity: [PF](https://pfonline.com/articles/how-to-limit-outgassing-on-castings).

### Inferences
- "Thick sections" is not stated as such in any source; heavy sections matter for dwell (heat-up time), per TIGER's "adjusted based on substrate thickness".

### Gaps
- No source found on vent holes in welded fabrications for powder coating (galvanizer vent/drain hole guidance exists in AGA design literature but was not read). No primary source for "thick sections trap gas".

## Claim 6: Fresh galvanizing white rust / zinc oxide weakens adhesion; how to remove

### Takeaway
PARTLY CONFIRMED. ASTM D7803 and AGA say zinc oxides must be removed and cause adhesion and blister problems; removal methods are sweep blast or phosphate/acid wash. Steel.org.au gives light brush/wire brush/vinegar for white rust in general, not specifically for powder.

### Cited Findings
- ASTM D7803: "The surface must be entirely free from visible metal oxides prior to powder coating"; weakly adhered oxides must be removed for adhesion and to avoid moisture entrapment causing blistering. — [ASTM D7803-25](https://store.astm.org/d7803-25.html)
- AGA: water vapor or air trapped in zinc oxides can "expand and penetrate the powder coating, causing blisters or voids"; sweep blast (SP 16) or mildly acidic phosphate wash to dissolve oxides; apply soon after so oxidation does not restart. — [AGA](https://galvanizeit.org/knowledgebase/article/powder-coating-adhesion-and-outgassing)
- Galvanizing Australia (steel.org.au) INGAL: light white rust brushed off or weathers off; moderate/severe: "Wire brush the affected area", white vinegar with nylon brush effective; the document does not discuss powder adhesion. — [White rust prevention and treatment](https://www.steel.org.au/getattachment/074d4981-2f9d-42d6-b772-e07e8186edac/White-rust-prevention-and-treatment_INGALSM3.pdf)
- Chromate passivation avoids white rust but GAA notes it is a passivation film on zinc — [GAA](https://www.steel.org.au/getattachment/44133ac4-bf76-4a27-9c29-120e519d3436/Powder-coating-over-galvanised-). Whether chromated surfaces are acceptable under D7803 was not verified (SP 16 sweep is the AGA route).

### Inferences
- For Indian shops with stored or monsoon-exposed work: sweep blast or zinc-suitable acid-etch pretreatment is the sourced route; wire-brush/vinegar is a general galvanizing-industry white rust fix and should be confirmed with the powder maker before use under powder.

### Gaps
- No powder-maker data sheet wording on white rust removal found (Interpon/PPG/Axalta/Jotun).

## Where sources disagree (key question)

### Takeaway
Main split: coat while the part is still warm (TIGER, PCI) versus cool fully then coat (AGA/ASTM, Products Finishing, Columbia, PF Liberto). Dwell time, offset above cure, and ceiling also differ. Data sheet wins.

### Cited Findings
- Coat while warm: TIGER says "Apply coating before cooling to prevent air re-entry into substrate porosity" — [TIGER blog](https://www.tiger-coatings.com/about/tiger-blog/outgassing-in-powder-coating); PF/Denerville: "Coat the part before it cools" — [PF](https://pfonline.com/articles/outgassing-and-powder-coating-defects). PCI: cool to within 25 F of ambient and apply while parts remain 25-30 F above ambient to prevent reabsorption of moisture and air. — [PCI](https://powdercoatedtough.com/News/ID/5239/Dealing-with-OutgassingRoot-Causes-and-Mitigation-Techniques)
- Cool then coat: PF die-cast "preheat... then cool before coating"; AGA: cool "to a temperature that allows application... in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions" while coating "as soon as possible". These are partly compatible with PCI (warm-to-cool but not hot).
- Iron casting TIGER article: cool thoroughly before blasting, optional second oven pass after blasting; Columbia: outgas before conversion coating; AGA/D7803: pre-bake after blasting/pretreatment. Order differs by substrate (castings are blasted after degas to avoid grit in pores; galvanized is blasted before).
- Pre-bake temperature/time: +15 to +20 C (TIGER/PF), +11-17 C (PCI), +28 C (PF die-cast), +36 C (AGA); times 15-30 min (Columbia), 45-50 min (TIGER castings), ~1 h (AGA), "same or longer than cure" (PF Liberto).
- Effectiveness: PF Talbert says preheating "very good to not effective at all"; pre-baking is "most popular" per PCI.
- Powder-maker TDS (Interpon APA151) route around pre-bake with an anti-gassing primer at 60-80 µm.

### Inferences
- A defensible Indian-shop rule: follow the powder TDS for part metal temperature; pre-bake at or above cure PMT (plus the maker's offset), hold until the part reaches temperature, and either coat while warm (still above ambient, per PCI) or follow the maker's cool-down instruction. Room-temperature cool-down raises re-adsorption risk in castings and zinc re-oxidation risk, per the sources above.
- Where sources disagree on numbers, the maker's TDS and AGA/ASTM D7803 should take precedence over generic trade articles.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head test data on warm vs cool application; disagreement is advice-versus-advice.
- Several sources are trade/distributor articles (Products Finishing, PCI magazine, Columbia Coatings) rather than powder-maker data sheets; only Interpon APA151 and TIGER/Teknos/Sherwin-Williams pages are manufacturer.
- GAA (Australia) document line "air temperatures of 250°C was found to be desirable, with an optimum operating temperature of 140°C" for a dry-off oven looks internally inconsistent; treat as unreliable until the original is read.
